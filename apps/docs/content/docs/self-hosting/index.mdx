The core of Ciele is open source under the **AGPL-3.0** license, and you can run
it yourself. The open-source edition is the whole product your visitors and
administrators use: the admin console, the chat widget, the [flow](/flows) router,
the [knowledge](/knowledge) and retrieval pipeline, and the operations surfaces —
[inbox](/operations/inbox), [improvements](/operations/improvements),
[insights](/operations/insights), and [alerts](/operations/alerts).

<Callout title="Same product, you operate it">
Every feature page in the main [Documentation](/) applies to a self-hosted
deployment. The difference is operational: you provide the infrastructure, the
database, and the AI provider keys, and you apply upgrades. The managed
alternative is [Ciele Cloud](/cloud).
</Callout>

## What you provide

- **A place to run it.** The apps are standard Next.js applications; anywhere that
  runs Node can host them.
- **A database.** Ciele stores its data in Postgres, using [Supabase](/self-hosting/database)
  for the database, authentication, and row-level security.
- **AI provider keys.** Assistants call an AI provider to generate answers; you
  bring your own keys. See [Configuration](/self-hosting/configuration).

## The license, briefly

AGPL-3.0 is an open-source license. You may run, study, modify, and share the
software. Its defining condition is the network clause: if you modify Ciele and
offer that modified version to others over a network, you must also offer those
users the corresponding source of your modified version. Running it unmodified,
or modifying it only for your own organization's internal use, does not trigger
that obligation. This page is a practical summary, not legal advice; the
authoritative terms are in the `LICENSE` file at the repository root.

If your organization would rather not take on AGPL obligations or the operating
work, [Ciele Cloud](/cloud) is the same core under a commercial arrangement.

## What is not in the open-source edition

A small set of commercial, managed-service features live outside the open-source
core and are not part of a self-hosted deployment. See
[The open-core boundary](/self-hosting/open-core-boundary) for the exact line.
